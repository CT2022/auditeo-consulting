Working Capital Finance

CMA Data — Credit Monitoring Arrangement

CMA Data is the standardised financial report mandated by RBI for all banks to assess working capital loan applications. It is the primary document for Cash Credit (CC), Overdraft (OD), Letter of Credit (LC) and working capital term loan applications. Banks use it to determine Maximum Permissible Bank Finance (MPBF) — i.e., how much they will lend.

💡 RBI Mandate: As per RBI guidelines, CMA data is compulsory for all working capital limits above ₹5 Lakhs. It presents 2 years of past actual financials + current year estimates + 5 years of future projections in a fixed bank-specified format.

The 6 Forms of CMA Data

FormNameWhat It Shows
Form IOperating StatementNet sales, cost of production/sales, gross profit, net profit — past 2–3 years actual + 5 years projected
Form IIAnalysis of Balance SheetAssets (fixed & current), liabilities (long-term & current), net worth — past & projected
Form IIIComparative Statement of CAs & CLsWorking capital gap — current assets vs current liabilities analysis to determine WC requirement
Form IVActual & Projected Fund FlowSources and uses of funds — shows how business is funding its operations & investments
Form VComputation of MPBFMaximum Permissible Bank Finance — Method I (working capital gap) & Method II (25% NWC rule)
Form VIKey Financial RatiosCurrent ratio, debt-equity, DSCR, net profit %, TOL/TNW — key ratios banks use for credit scoring

What We Prepare in CMA Data

  • Complete 6-Form CMA in bank's required format (RBI/IBA prescribed)
  • Realistic financial projections based on industry benchmarks & your business model
  • MPBF computation — Method I & Method II to justify your CC/OD requirement
  • Working capital cycle analysis — inventory days, debtor days, creditor days
  • Key financial ratios — current ratio, DSCR, TOL/TNW — optimised for bank acceptance
  • Supporting narrative — business overview, market position & growth rationale
  • Sensitivity analysis — projections under conservative, base & optimistic scenarios
  • Existing CMA revision — enhancement of CC/OD limits for growing businesses
  • CA certification & signature on all CMA forms

When Do You Need CMA Data?

🆕 New Cash Credit (CC) Limit

First-time application for working capital CC limit from a bank. CMA shows your WC requirement and repayment capacity.

📈 CC/OD Enhancement

Increasing your existing CC or OD limit when business has grown. Updated CMA shows higher projected turnover & WC need.

🏦 Bank Transfer / Switch

Moving your CC account from one bank to another. New bank requires fresh CMA with takeover justification.

📋 Annual Review / Renewal

Banks require annual CMA renewal with updated actuals for continuing CC/OD limits — typically due every April–June.

Capital Expenditure Finance

Detailed Project Report (DPR) for Term Loans

A Detailed Project Report (DPR) is required for term loans — loans taken for setting up a new business, expanding existing facilities, or purchasing machinery & equipment. Unlike CMA Data (which focuses on working capital), a DPR presents the complete business case for a long-term investment to the bank's credit committee.

💡 A standard DPR for an MSME term loan is 30–50 pages. A large project DPR extends to 80–120 pages with technical annexures. We prepare both — tailored to your loan amount, bank & project type.

Components of a Complete DPR

📋 Executive Summary

Project overview, promoter background, loan requirement & key financial highlights in 2 pages

👤 Promoter Profile

Educational & professional background, industry experience, net worth statement & guarantor details

🏭 Project Description

Nature of business, product/service details, manufacturing process or service delivery model

📍 Location & Site

Factory/office location, land details, layout plan, access to raw materials, power & water

⚙️ Technical Details

Machinery list with quotations, installed capacity, raw material sourcing & technology description

📊 Market Analysis

Target market, demand-supply analysis, competitive landscape, pricing strategy & sales projections

💰 Project Cost & Means of Finance

Total project cost breakup (land, building, machinery, WC margin) & funding mix (own funds + bank loan)

📈 Financial Projections (7 years)

Year-wise P&L, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow statement with all assumptions clearly stated

🔁 Loan Repayment Schedule

EMI/instalment schedule matching bank's repayment norms — quarterly or monthly as applicable

⚖️ Break-Even Analysis

Break-even point (units & value), margin of safety & contribution margin analysis

📉 DSCR & IRR

Debt Service Coverage Ratio (min 1.33x for banks), Internal Rate of Return & Net Present Value

🔍 Sensitivity Analysis

Impact of 10–20% variation in sales, cost or interest rates on financial viability & loan repayment

Agriculture & Rural

NABARD & Agri-Business Project Reports

NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development) refinances banks for agriculture & rural enterprise loans. A NABARD-compliant project report must satisfy both the bank's credit team AND NABARD's refinance criteria — covering unit economics, input-output ratios, cost per unit & financial viability specific to agri-based enterprises.

💡 NABARD project reports are more detailed than standard DPRs. They require agri-specific analysis: production parameters, input cost break-up, seasonal cash flow, soil reports (for farm projects), and government scheme linkage (KCC, RKVY, PKVY etc.).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a Project Report and CMA Data? +
A Project Report (DPR) presents the complete business plan — project cost, machinery, market analysis, financial projections, break-even & DSCR. Banks use it for term loan appraisal for new projects or expansion. CMA Data is the standardised financial format (6 forms) used by banks for working capital limits (Cash Credit, OD). It shows MPBF — how much working capital the bank should lend based on your operating cycle. Most banks ask for both when sanctioning a new project with both term loan AND working capital.
Which government scheme is best for a new small business? +
It depends on your project cost and category. PMEGP is best for new manufacturing (up to ₹50L) or service (up to ₹20L) businesses — it gives 15%–35% subsidy. MUDRA is ideal for very small businesses up to ₹10 Lakhs — fast, collateral-free, minimal documentation. CGTMSE provides collateral-free guarantee up to ₹2 Crore for MSMEs that have good financials but no property to pledge. Stand-Up India is exclusively for SC/ST and women entrepreneurs for loans ₹10L–₹1 Crore. We help you identify the right scheme and prepare the correct format report.
How long does it take to prepare a project report? +
MUDRA/Basic reports: 2–3 working days. PMEGP/CGTMSE/Standard DPR: 3–5 working days. Large project DPR (₹1 Crore+): 5–7 working days. NABARD/Agri DPR: 4–6 working days. Timeline depends on how quickly you provide the required information. We send you a simple questionnaire to fill — takes about 30–45 minutes. Once we have all data, we start immediately.
Will the bank definitely sanction the loan with your project report? +
No one can guarantee loan sanction — that depends on your creditworthiness, CIBIL score, collateral, bank policy & the banker's assessment. What we guarantee is a professionally prepared, CA-certified report in the exact format the bank requires, with realistic financial projections that clearly demonstrate repayment capacity. A strong report eliminates report-related rejection reasons and significantly improves your chances. We also provide post-submission support — if the bank asks for additional information or revised projections, we assist at no extra charge.
What is MPBF and how is it calculated in CMA Data? +
MPBF — Maximum Permissible Bank Finance — is the maximum working capital loan a bank can give you as per RBI guidelines. It is computed in two ways: Method I = 75% × (Current Assets − Current Liabilities excluding bank borrowings). Method II = 75% × (Current Assets) − (Current Liabilities excluding bank borrowings). RBI mandates banks use the higher of the two. We compute MPBF accurately in CMA Form V to ensure your CC/OD limit request is fully justified.
Can I get a PMEGP loan if I already run a business? +
PMEGP is primarily for new micro-enterprises. However, a 2nd PMEGP loan is available for well-performing existing PMEGP/MUDRA units for expansion/upgradation. The subsidy for 2nd PMEGP is 15% (for General in both Urban & Rural) and 20% for Special category. Your existing unit must be a minimum 3 years old and performing well. We prepare both 1st and 2nd PMEGP project reports.
Is CIBIL score checked for MUDRA loans? +
For MUDRA Shishu (up to ₹50,000), many lenders are flexible on CIBIL and may not check it strictly. For Kishore, Tarun and Tarun Plus, banks generally do check CIBIL and prefer a score above 650. For PMEGP and CGTMSE, CIBIL is important and poor credit history can cause rejection. If you have a low CIBIL score, we can advise on improving it before applying and recommend the right lender for your situation.
Do you prepare project reports for industries in Jharkhand specifically? +
Yes — we are based in Ranchi, Jharkhand and have deep expertise in local industries like mining, steel, agro-processing, handicrafts, forest produce, dairy, poultry, and service businesses across Jharkhand. We know the district-specific norms of major banks operating in Jharkhand — SBI, Bank of Baroda, Jharkhand Gramin Bank, PNB, Central Bank — and prepare reports per their specific underwriting preferences. We also prepare reports for Jharkhand state government schemes like JKVY, MSME Policy 2021 and JSLPS SHG-bank linkage programmes.
